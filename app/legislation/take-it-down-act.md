The TAKE IT DOWN Act makes it a federal crime to knowingly publish nonconsensual intimate images of real people — including AI-generated deepfake pornography — without consent. It also requires covered online platforms to build a removal process and take down such content, and known copies of it, within 48 hours of a valid victim request, with the FTC empowered to enforce compliance.

It's the only bill on this page that has completed the full legislative process: introduced by Sen. Cruz in January 2025, it passed the Senate unanimously and the House 409-2, and President Trump signed it into law that May as Public Law 119-12. Its criminal provisions took effect immediately; covered platforms had one year to stand up notice-and-removal procedures, and the FTC confirmed it began enforcing that requirement on May 19, 2026, sending compliance letters to major platforms including Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok, X, Snapchat, and Reddit, with penalties of up to roughly $53,000 per violation.
