The AI Talent Act would authorize federal agencies to establish "technology and AI talent teams" — recruiters, hiring-assessment specialists, and subject-matter experts — to speed up competitive-service hiring for AI-related roles, and would direct the Office of Personnel Management to stand up a government-wide Federal Technology and AI Talent Team offering shared hiring certificates, cross-agency candidate pools, common training, and a shared technology platform. It extends similar talent-team authority to other high-need technical fields (cybersecurity, IT, data science) alongside AI, and phases out reliance on automated self-assessments in hiring after five years.

Unlike most bills on this tracker, the AI Talent Act binds nobody outside government — it's entirely a federal-workforce and civil-service-hiring mechanism aimed at helping agencies compete with the private sector for AI talent, not a regulatory measure touching AI developers, deployers, or consumers. It was introduced as true House/Senate companions on the same day by Rep. Sara Jacobs (D-CA) and Sen. Andy Kim (D-NJ), with bipartisan cosponsors Rep. Jay Obernolte (R-CA), Rep. Pat Fallon (R-TX), Rep. Shontel Brown (D-OH), and Sen. Jon Husted (R-OH), and has drawn endorsements from Encode AI, ITIF, and the Bipartisan Policy Center.

**A note on the Senate companion:** some secondary sources have cited S. 5307 as the Senate companion to this bill. That is incorrect — S. 5307 is a distinct bill, the "Artificial Intelligence and Innovation Talent Act," which addresses AI-related immigration policy rather than federal hiring authority. The true, same-day Senate companion is S. 3410.
