The SANDBOX Act would direct the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy to run a federal AI "regulatory sandbox," letting companies apply for temporary waivers from specified federal regulations to test AI products or development methods on a limited basis. Waivers would run in renewable two-year terms up to a maximum of ten years, require 72-hour incident reporting, and the program overall would sunset after twelve years, with annual reports back to Congress.

It has drawn a fairly clean industry-versus-labor split in the opposition and support it has attracted: groups like the AFL-CIO, Public Citizen, and the Center for Progressive Reform have sent opposition letters, while the Software & Information Industry Association, the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, and the Information Technology Industry Council back it. A scheduled markup in Sen. Cruz's own committee was postponed in late July 2026 for lack of bipartisan agreement, with a second attempt expected in September.
