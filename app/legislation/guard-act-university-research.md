The GUARD Act (Growing University AI Research for Defense Act) would authorize the Secretary of Defense to designate eligible U.S. host institutions — universities and senior military colleges — to establish and run AI research institutes focused on national-security and defense applications, with five-year renewable funding, mandated cross-sector partnerships, and workforce-development components aimed at building the AI talent pipeline the Pentagon needs to compete with China.

Rather than advancing as a standalone bill, this policy has already become law: its substance was incorporated into Section 224 of the FY2026 National Defense Authorization Act (S. 1071), which the President signed on December 18, 2025, establishing the "National Security and Defense Artificial Intelligence Institute" program. Both the House bill's sponsor, Rep. Ronny Jackson (R-TX), and the Senate bill's sponsor, Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX), have publicly claimed credit for securing this language in the enacted defense bill. The standalone bills, H.R. 5466 and S. 3454, remain technically pending at committee referral, but GovTrack's own tracking language confirms their provisions were "incorporated into other bills which were enacted" — meaning no further independent action on the standalone text is expected.

**A note on naming — a three-way collision.** "GUARD Act" is used as a short title by at least three unrelated 119th Congress bills tracked on this page or elsewhere:

1. **This bill** — H.R. 5466 / S. 3454, "Growing University AI Research for Defense Act," Jackson/Cornyn — a defense-research-funding authorization, now enacted via the FY2026 NDAA.
2. **The chatbot child-safety GUARD Act** — S. 3062 / H.R. 8623, "Guidelines for User Age-verification and Responsible Dialogue Act," Hawley/Blumenthal — tracked separately on this page, requiring AI chatbot age verification and banning minors from AI companion chatbots.
3. **The robotics national-security GUARD Act** — H.R. 9129, "Guarding the U.S. against Adversarial Robotics Dominance Act," addressing Chinese robotics and the FCC Covered List — tracked separately.

All three are genuinely distinct bills sponsored by different members addressing entirely different subjects; they share only the acronym.
