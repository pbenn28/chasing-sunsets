The Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, announced by Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) and Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX) on September 3, 2026, would permanently ban the development or deployment of "artificial superintelligence" — defined to include systems that match or exceed human cognitive performance across a broad range of domains, or that are capable of planning or executing the disempowerment of humanity, including undermining the U.S. government or subverting shutdown commands. Pending that permanent ban's implementation, it would temporarily pause development of "advanced frontier AI" systems until a newly created cabinet-level federal AI agency issues safety rules and stands up a model-review process.

The new agency — unnamed in materials reviewed — would monitor frontier AI systems across their lifecycle, oversee removal of dangerous capabilities, and oversee destruction of any system found to be superintelligent, advised by an outside Artificial Intelligence Advisory Board of AI experts. Penalties are severe: forced corporate dissolution (a "corporate death penalty," explicitly analogized by sponsors to penalties for illegal nuclear weapons development) and up to 20 years' imprisonment for individuals. The proposal also directs U.S. policy toward international agreements and export controls to prevent superintelligence development abroad.

As of this writing, no bill number has been assigned in either chamber — the sponsors' own release describes the legislation in future terms ("to introduce"), and no matching bill appears in a targeted congress.gov search for "superintelligence" bills in the 119th Congress. AI-policy researchers quoted in coverage of the announcement have also disputed whether "superintelligence" is a coherently definable term for statutory purposes, an open question that would need to be resolved in any actual bill text.
