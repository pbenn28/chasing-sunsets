The Future of Artificial Intelligence Innovation Act of 2026 would formally authorize the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) inside NIST/Commerce to develop voluntary AI standards, metrics, performance benchmarks, and transparency-documentation guidelines. It would fund public-private testbed partnerships among NIST, NSF, DOE, and industry to build security-risk evaluation tools, launch grand-challenge prize competitions in areas like quantum computing, microelectronics, and border-security applications of AI, direct federal science agencies to publish curated datasets for AI research, and establish an international coalition with allied governments on AI standards -- alongside a reporting requirement on China's WTO compliance and AI-sector risks.

The bill is a reintroduction led by Sen. Todd Young (R-IN) with Sens. Maria Cantwell (D-WA), Marsha Blackburn (R-TN), and John Hickenlooper (D-CO), introduced February 26, 2026 and referred to Senate Commerce, which ordered a Star Print in March 2026. It is a genuinely distinct, standalone bill from the Great American AI Act discussion draft (tracked separately) rather than a rename of it — but both bills converge on the same institutional target: this bill statutorily authorizes and funds CAISI directly, while GAAIA's own frontier-governance title would establish essentially the same center from a broader, developer-obligation-heavy comprehensive framework. If both ever advanced, their CAISI provisions would likely need to be reconciled rather than treated as unrelated.
