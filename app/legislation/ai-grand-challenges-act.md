The AI Grand Challenges Act of 2026 directs the National Science Foundation to establish an AI Grand Challenges Program, authorizing the NSF Director to identify "grand challenges" and run prize competitions — with purses of at least $1 million — for AI research and development addressing problems in health, energy, environment, national security, materials science, and cybersecurity, as well as AI-specific issues like bias mitigation and explainability. The bill also directs a dedicated $10 million challenge track, run jointly with OSTP and NIH, aimed at AI-enabled cancer detection, diagnostics, and treatment breakthroughs. It requires NSF to report to the relevant House and Senate committees within 60 days of awarding a prize and biennially thereafter. The bill is a bipartisan, bicameral reintroduction (Reps. Lieu and Obernolte in the House; Sens. Booker, Rounds, and Heinrich in the Senate) of a similar bill from the 118th Congress that did not advance past committee referral. As of early September 2026, both the House and Senate versions remain in committee with no markup or further action reported since introduction.
