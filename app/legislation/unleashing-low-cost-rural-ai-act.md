The bill directs the Secretary of Energy to designate a National Laboratory to study AI and data-center growth's impact on U.S. energy supply, prioritizing rural and remote areas. The study would cover infrastructure upgrade needs, the feasibility of alternative energy sources (nuclear, geothermal, hydro, solar, wind, battery storage, carbon capture), co-location of AI/data-center sites with power generation, and effects on energy costs, reliability, and land/water use — plus, within that study, means to expedite NEPA review or permitting for AI/data-center sites and associated energy infrastructure. A report to Congress would be due 180 days after enactment. Reps. Costa (D-CA) and Moore (R-UT) lead a bipartisan sponsorship tied to concerns about rural ratepayers absorbing AI-driven electricity cost increases (citing a PJM estimate of roughly $9.3 billion in added grid costs).
