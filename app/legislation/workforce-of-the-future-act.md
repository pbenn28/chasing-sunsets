The bill directs the Secretaries of Labor, Commerce, and Education to jointly study and report on AI's impact on the economy and workforce — identifying which industries and jobs are most affected, how job quality will change, what skills workers will need, and recommendations for preparing the workforce. It authorizes $160 million in Department of Education grants to expand access to emerging and advanced-technology education (teacher training/recruitment, learning materials, closing access gaps for underrepresented groups) and $90 million in Department of Labor grants for training workers in AI-impacted industries, including both displacement-prevention training and rapid-response support for workers who lose jobs to AI. The bill is endorsed by AFT, Anthropic, Microsoft, OpenAI, and SAG-AFTRA, among others.

As of 2026-09-09, the bill remains at its original December 2025 committee referrals in both chambers, with 10 House cosponsors (all Democratic) and no markup or floor action recorded.
