The China AI Power Report Act would require the Secretaries of Commerce and State to jointly produce a recurring report to Congress assessing the People's Republic of China's artificial-intelligence capabilities: chip design and production, semiconductor fab capacity and supply chains, frontier model development and deployment, People's Liberation Army military AI applications, AI-enabled surveillance of Uyghurs and other ethnic minorities, and how well existing U.S. export controls are holding up against circumvention. The report would carry an unclassified public component plus an optional classified annex, with mandatory classified and unclassified briefings to Congress within 90 days of submission.

The House version, H.R. 6275, is the more advanced vehicle — introduced by Rep. James Moylan (R-GU) with Reps. Eugene Vindman (D-VA) and Bill Huizenga (R-MI) as original cosponsors, it cleared the House Foreign Affairs Committee by a lopsided 47-0 vote in January 2026. The Senate companion, S. 5382, came later: Sen. Jon Husted (R-OH) introduced it in August 2026 with Sen. Mark Warner (D-VA) as a bipartisan cosponsor, as part of a broader package of China-accountability bills, but it has not yet had any committee action.

This bill sits alongside other China-focused reporting bills already tracked here, including the China Advanced Technology Monitoring Act (DoD report on PRC semiconductor manufacturing) and the AI OVERWATCH Act (a chip-export congressional-review mechanism) — together forming a cluster of China-AI-oversight bills moving through the 119th Congress's national-security committees.
