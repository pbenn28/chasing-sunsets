The bill's official long title is "To provide for a limitation on the obligation of funds for certain immigration enforcement purposes until a report on the use of databases by immigration officers is submitted." It blocks ICE from obligating funds for expanded surveillance tools or additional staffing/hiring until the agency reports to Congress on how it uses databases and biometric technologies — including facial recognition — covering who is targeted, how long data is retained, and whether practices comply with First and Fourth Amendment protections and state/local law. It separately freezes ICE's contract with Clearview AI, the facial-recognition vendor, until Congress receives assurances that peaceful observers, bystanders, and protesters are not being swept into immigration biometric databases. Rep. Chellie Pingree (D-ME) introduced the bill after reports that ICE agents in South Portland, Maine photographed and tracked the license plates of bystanders filming enforcement actions, reportedly threatening a "domestic terrorist" database entry.

**Overlap with the ICE Out of Our Faces Act (S. 3779 / H.R. 7363):** both bills target ICE's use of facial recognition, but through substantively different mechanisms. The ICE Out of Our Faces Act is a categorical statutory ban — it prohibits ICE/CBP from acquiring or using facial recognition and other biometric surveillance outright, mandates deletion of existing data within 30 days, makes unlawfully obtained evidence inadmissible, and creates a private right of action plus state-AG enforcement. The Stop ICE Intimidation Act does not ban facial recognition; it is a funding-conditionality and disclosure mechanism that freezes money for surveillance expansion and one named vendor contract until ICE reports to Congress, unlocking again once that report is filed. The two bills are complementary rather than duplicative — Pingree's bill would not be redundant if enacted alongside the Markey/Jayapal ban, since it also reaches ICE hiring/staffing funds that the categorical ban does not address.
