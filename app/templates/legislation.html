How to read this

Each bill is scored against a written rubric across several independent dimensions, then combined into the numbers below. These are rubric-driven scores with a documented methodology, not one-off editorial judgment — but reasonable people can still disagree with the rubric itself, and every score has a full breakdown and rationale in the bill's expanded entry.

Safety score (S)
Deregulation / accelerationSafety / restriction

A composite of how much the bill restricts frontier AI developers, adjusted for how much it preempts state authority, how much government oversight capacity it builds, and how much it constrains (or accelerates) the physical buildout of compute and energy — data centers, permitting, siting, power. Positive means more safety/restriction, negative means more deregulation/acceleration. Each dimension's contribution to S has its own adjustable weight below the chart — moving one can flip a bill's sign, which is often the whole story.

Impact

How much would change if the bill were enacted — combining how much of the industry it reaches, how far it pushes any single axis from neutral, how enforceable it is, and how much precedent it would set. This measures magnitude, not direction: a maximally deregulatory bill and a maximally restrictive one can score the same.

P(enact)

A modeled probability that the bill is ultimately enacted into law — anchored to external models like GovTrack's prognosis tool where available, otherwise estimated from historical base rates for bills at a comparable stage. Bills are sorted by Expected Impact (Impact × P(enact)) by default, which surfaces bills that are both consequential and plausible rather than either extreme alone.

Every dimension that feeds S has its own weight, adjustable with the sliders below the chart — including the export controls / geopolitical dimension, which is tracked and shown in each bill's breakdown but weighted at 0 by default, since it doesn't cleanly map to the safety/acceleration spectrum (there are coherent arguments for tightening or loosening controls on either side of that debate). Raise its weight above 0 if you want to fold it in.

Scoring rubric & equations

Six independent dimensions, each scored against a written ladder, roll up into two composites. Full ladders and the reasoning behind each are in the project's scoring rubric doc — this is the short version.

A · Frontier developer stringency (−5…+5, signed)
Conduct standards on developers only — training moratoria, audits, disclosure, incident reporting. Physical-input constraints (compute, energy, siting) belong on F or D instead, never here.
B · Preemption posture (−5…+3, signed)
Does the bill preserve, narrow, or eliminate states' authority to regulate AI independently?
C · Governance capacity (−3…+5, signed)
Does it build or strip the government's own capacity to observe, evaluate, and enforce — distinct from capacity to build AI?
D · External / geopolitical (−3…+5, signed)
Export controls and chip-access posture. Weighted at 0 by default (see above) but always shown.
F · Capability buildout constraint (−5…+5, signed)
Domestic compute/energy chokepoints — data centers, permitting, siting, power allocation. A moratorium on facilities is +5 here, not on A, because it constrains an input rather than developer conduct.
E · Consumer / near-term harm (0…5, unsigned, impact-only)
Deepfakes, NCII, companion bots, discrimination, election integrity. Never enters the safety composite — it's a different kind of stringency.

S = clamp(wA·A + wB·B + wC·C + wD·D + wF·F, −5, +5)
defaults: wA={{ scoring_weights.w_A }}, wB={{ scoring_weights.w_B }}, wC={{ scoring_weights.w_C }}, wD={{ scoring_weights.w_D }}, wF={{ scoring_weights.w_F }}

Impact = 10 × (0.35·R + 0.30·Depth + 0.20·Ef + 0.15·P), each component normalized 0–1
ExpectedImpact = Impact × P(enact) — the default sort key

S is never stored — only the raw axis values are, so the weight sliders below the chart recompute everything live without re-scoring a single bill.

How the probability model works

P(enact) isn't a vibes number — it's two modeled probabilities per bill: p(committee), the chance it advances out of committee, and p(enact), the chance it's ultimately signed into law. The chart and default sort use p(enact).

Where an external model exists — most often GovTrack.us's published "prognosis" for a specific bill — the score is anchored to it directly, and the bill's rationale says so explicitly. Where no external model is available, the estimate is built from historical base rates for bills at a comparable stage (e.g. the share of House bills that clear committee at all in a given Congress), adjusted up or down for that bill's cosponsor count, bipartisan pairing, committee-chair alignment, and recent news-cycle attention. Bills that have already passed or been signed are simply set to 1.0 — there's no remaining uncertainty to model.

Research effort was allocated unevenly on purpose: bills further along in the legislative process got individual deep-research passes (live status checks, GovTrack cross-referencing), while early-stage bills with little momentum were scored in batches from their own text plus lighter live lookups. A bill's confidence field (shown in its expanded entry) reflects how that bill was scored.

Every bill also records a text_source: whether its axis scores were verified against the bill's actual introduced text (fetched from congress.gov) or only against a curated summary. A summary can omit a provision — most often a preemption or savings clause — that the real bill text contains, so any bill not yet verified against full text is capped at low confidence and left off the scatter chart above until it's checked, even if the underlying scores turn out to be correct.
